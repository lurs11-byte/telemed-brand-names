Naming criteria & considerations

Reference for the team and for Claude across future rounds — collected from the original brief, from patterns that repeated across the 130 rejected names, and from concrete legal lessons. Check a new idea against this before it goes into the system, don't just go on gut feel.

Core principles (from the brief)

  • Market: Germany first, Europe next. The US gets a separate brand — don't fold US constraints into this evaluation.
  • Category: multi-category consumer healthcare / telemedicine. The name has to survive future lines (Weight, Longevity, Hormones, Diagnostics), not lock into one.
  • Not: a hospital · pharma-coded · a cold clinic · B2B software · a doctor marketplace · an AI company. Also not the reverse — a legitimately medical-sounding name is fine.
  • Target zone: consumer brand × credible medicine × modern European healthcare — between "too clinical" and "too generic-wellness."

Germany evaluation criteria (per name)

  • Pronunciation — can a German consumer read it correctly at first sight, with no guidance?
  • German meaning — does the name, or part of it, mean something in German (good or bad)? (e.g. "bis" = "until" — killed Wellbis)
  • Associations — could the spelling or sound trigger an unintended association?
  • Medical credibility — would a German consumer trust this brand with an actual medical treatment?
  • Ownability — distinctive enough to become a recognizable brand? Don't reward a generic word just because it's easy to pronounce — this was the single most common rejection reason across both rounds.

Concrete legal / brand lessons (these actually happened)

Vivra ≈ Viagra — a phonetic (not string) collision with a heavily protected pharma trademark. Check phonetic proximity, not just exact string matches.
PHRM ≈ the PHRM Nasdaq ticker (Pharming Group N.V.). Check stock tickers too, not just trademarks and companies.
Beacon / Harbor sit directly on existing health companies (Beacon Health Options). Search "name + health/care company" before a name goes to KEEP.
Domains — reality check: of the 30 live candidates actually checked (real RDAP/WHOIS, not guessing), 30/30 .com were taken, .de and .eu were almost always taken too. Only Medwill and Clinyx came back available. A short, catchy English word is almost always already squatted — budget for buying a domain or plan an alternate TLD strategy from the start.

How to generate new ideas (the extension/combination method — e.g. Vivo Med)

  1. Take a root that already scored well (KEEP, high Germany Score, good ownability) — e.g. Vivo.
  2. Test combining it with a category word (Med, Care, Health) — does it add medical credibility without losing distinctiveness, or does it just get heavier and more generic?
  3. Known risk: "Medical + Consumer" already has several rejections for "generic / B2B / too playful" (NovoMed, Medvian, Medozo). A new combination in this world starts with a higher burden of proof, not a lower one.
  4. Every new combination enters as MAYBE, never straight to KEEP, until it's been fully scored.

What the sub-scores mean

Germany Score (0–10, composite) · Medical Trust · Consumer Appeal · Memorability · Ownability · Multi-category Fit — all live on each name's profile. A new name that hasn't been scored on all of them stays MAYBE on purpose.
